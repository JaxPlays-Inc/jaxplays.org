[[production:2026 Once Upon a One More Time|*Once Upon a One More Time*]] at [[theatre:Arts Collective Theatre]] is an absolute blast - a spectacle that feels like a millennial's ultimate fever dream. From the moment the iconic beats drop, this feminist fairytale electrifies the stage.<!--more-->

Co-directed with visual panache by [[person:Lee Hamby]] (who also designs the striking costumes) and [[person:Jocelyn Geronimo]] (who stars as Sleeping Beauty), and featuring immaculate wig design by [[person:Phedre Delinois]], the production is a feast for the senses. [[person:Allyson Mecca]]'s choreography drives the production with a kinetic punch - bursting with high-energy pop discipline, expertly maintained by dance captain [[person:Arielle Bryant]] (who also shines as Rapunzel). Backed by [[person:Christian Nyman]]'s crisp music di rection, [[person:Susan Roche]]'s sound design, props design by [[person:Julie Harrington]] and Hamby, visual design by [[person:Destiny Golden]], and stage management by [[person:Bethany Powell]], this show operates at peak performance.

The narrative flips classic tropes on their head: storybook heroines like Cinderella, Snow White, Rapunzel, Sleeping Beauty, Belle and the Little Mermaid spend their days dutifully repeating their traditional tales for a bedtime-reading child. But Cinderella senses something is missing from her "happily ever after" with Prince Charming. When the rogue O.F.G. slips her a copy of [[w:Betty Friedan]]'s [[w:The Feminine Mystique|*The Feminine Mystique*]], the princesses undergo an awakening, realizing that waiting around to be rescued or kissing a prince is not their only path to fulfillment.

There is not a single weak link in this cast. [[person:Christopher Watson]] anchors the story perfectly as the Narrator, delivering a deliciously theatrical, controlling presence. [[person:Amy Allen Farmer]] takes the stage as the Notorious O.F.G. (a role double-cast with [[person:Cathy OBrien|Cathy O'Brien]]), bringing spark, warmth and comedic flair. Young [[person:Kennedy Swartz]] brings wonder and sweetness to the Little Girl, effortlessly holding her own in intermittent scenes throughout the night before rocking out with star quality in her final moments.

The leading ladies form an absolute powerhouse unit, anchored by [[person:Julie Harrington]]'s heartfelt, resolute Cinderella and [[person:Rashawnda Campbell]]'s wonderfully commanding Snow White. [[person:Samantha Wicklund]] reigns supreme with delicious theatrical villainy as the Stepmother, while [[person:Kanesha Mitchell]] (Belinda) and [[person:Angelee Ramos]] (Betany) bring sharp comic timing and dynamic stage energy to their featured moments. Rounding out this stellar fairytale lineup, each actor delivers remarkably distinct character work - including [[person:Joanna Berry]]'s vibrant Little Mermaid, Geronimo's dreamily funny Sleeping Beauty, Bryant's spirited Rapunzel, [[person:Ashley Humphrey]]'s fierce Esmeralda, [[person:Lauren Manacup]]'s enchanting Belle, [[person:Jennifer OBrien|Jennifer O'Brien]]'s delightful Princess and the Pea, and [[person:Ashley Gibson]]'s spunky Gretel.

Matching that energy step-for-step are the featured gentlemen, who are all incredible. Whether executing choreography as a unified royal squad or shining in individual features, their dancing, timing and presence are impeccable. [[person:Ethan Edward Fisher]]'s Prince Charming hits every single millennial heartthrob vibe with precision - from the tongue-in-cheek humor and signature smirk to the sleek sunglasses and effortless pop-star moves, his performance is an absolute scream.

[[person:Gary Baker]]'s turn as Clumsy is endearingly sweet and genuinely funny, making him completely impossible not to adore. [[person:Darryl J Beamon]] (Prince Erudite), [[person:Phedre Delinois]] (Prince Mischievous), [[person:Jonathan Leonard]] (Prince Suave), [[person:Clayton Riddley]] (Prince Brawny) and [[person:Iaan Quintanilla]] (Prince Affable) bring charisma and stellar moves to their respective princes, anchoring the ensemble with fantastic skill.

The company operates with precision throughout the score. "Lucky" shines with expressive vocal detail, the Stepmother, Stepsisters and Cinderella bring down the house with "Work B\*tch," and Charming leads a show-stopping "Circus." With "Oops!... I Did It Again" delivering sharp comedic timing and "Womanizer" showcasing choreographic energy, Act I hits one high mark after another.

Act II kicks off with a bold visual dynamic - without giving too much away, audience members will walk out wanting a pair of Snow White's sparkly red boots. One of the high points of Act II is the production number "Toxic," which effortlessly weaves together design, movement and musical elements into a masterclass sequence featuring the Stepmother, the Narrator, and dynamic features by Humphrey, Gibson, O'Brien, Bryant, Berry and Manacup.

Fueled by [[w:Once_Upon_a_One_More_Time|Jon Hartmere]]'s clever book and the pop catalog of [[w:Britney Spears]], the momentum never wavers. By the time the show rolls through "Stronger," "Gimme More" and the finale "Till the World Ends," the energy in the theatre is off the charts. Arts Collective Theatre crafts a fun night out that leaves audience members dancing all the way to their cars.
