[[production:2026 Come From Away 2|Come From Away]] is one of the few shows I passionately believe every human being should see at least once. Its title derives from a Newfoundland expression: A "come from away" is anyone considered an outsider. And on Sept. 11, 2001, there was quite an influx of come-from-aways to that small Canadian island.<!--more-->

After the attacks, 38 planes carrying nearly 7,000 passengers were diverted to the town of Gander, Newfoundland. The residents themselves wondered, "Why Gander?" The stark answer was, "Because we have a lot less to lose." Completely unprepared for thousands of unexpected guests, the community banded together to house, feed and care for 7,000 complete strangers. In the process, they forged lifelong friendships and inspired lasting gratitude.

I had the pleasure of seeing [[theatre:Saltwater Performing Arts]] bring *Come From Away* to life on its stage during the sold-out closing matinee. The electricity in the room was palpable as the cast emerged from the wings onto the darkened stage, stomping in unison and chanting the hypnotic words, "I am an islander." Chills! From that opening moment, I knew we were in for an amazing show.

The experience was even more powerful because the stage was mere feet from the audience. The audio was well balanced and clear, while the cast's frenetic energy and remarkable versatility kept us completely mesmerized. Each performer took on the demanding task of portraying multiple characters while little more than a collection of simple chairs transformed before our eyes into airplane seats, a traveling bus, a dining room table and countless other locations.

Director and musical director Megan Friese-Kaufman and choreographer Lisa Allen Brooks deserve a standing ovation. The cast seamlessly shifted from passengers to islanders to airport employees, with costume changes, lighting and chair reconfigurations happening so organically that the audience barely noticed the transitions. The creative team also wisely invested in dialect coach [[person:Rachel Kelsey]], and that attention to detail paid off in the believability the cast brought to characters of varying nationalities.

Beyond the strong acting, Kelsey was a standout songbird as she portrayed the flight captain. Her performance beautifully balanced the vulnerability of a woman yearning for news of home with the composed strength of a captain carrying the weight of responsibility for her passengers and crew. On the opposite end of the spectrum, who could forget [[person:Renee LaCroix]]'s drunken rendition of "My Heart Will Go On" while standing atop the airplane seats?

Kelsey later revealed her comedic range as Annette, whose increasingly outlandish fantasies were hilariously brought to life by Steven Jones' sexy maraca shake, Chance Davis' smoldering "I'll clean your bathrooms" and Johnny Mandt's mysterious, sexy pilot captain. LaCroix was the perfect scene partner as Beulah, Annette's fellow schoolteacher. In a wonderful real-life connection, LaCroix has even been communicating with one of the women who inspired Beulah after the woman began liking her Instagram posts about the production. These moments provided much-needed levity and were some of the funniest highlights of the production.

While these lighter moments offered relief for the audience, tension continued to boil among the stranded passengers. Far from home with no end in sight, they worried about their loved ones, struggled with the uncertainty of how long they would remain stranded and eventually saw angst spill over into fighting within the shelters.

Thankfully, the citizens of Gander invited the passengers to the local pub for a raucous ceremony to become honorary Newfoundlanders. The show's most joyful scene featured Diane (Rachel Wise), Nick ([[person:Ken Johnson]]), Kevin (Davis) and Bob (Mandt) participating in a screech-in ceremony officiated by Claude (Erik Nesteruk). Jubilation abounded with Celtic dancing, yellow fisherman caps and, of course, kissing a cod.

By the end, *Come From Away* had brought tears to my eyes and restored my faith in humanity. The Saltwater Performing Arts team created an outstanding production. To me, the mark of a phenomenal production is when the sets, costumes and lighting are stripped to their simplest elements, yet the actors tell the story so powerfully that your imagination fills in every blank. You become completely captivated, immersed and swept away, and this cast accomplished exactly that.

While St. Marys, Georgia, may be a longer drive than some of our local theatres, it is a delightful little town with the seaside charm of a Hallmark movie. The scenic marshes also provide the perfect setting for enjoying some fresh seafood after the performance.

Although the show's regular run has ended, Saltwater Performing Arts will present a special 60-minute version Sept. 11, with free admission for first responders. Buy your tickets soon. You will not want to miss this extraordinary show.
