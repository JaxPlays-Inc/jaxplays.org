With a sharp book by [[w:Terrence McNally]] and an infectious score by [[w:Marc Shaiman]] and [[w:Scott Wittman]], [[theatre:Limelight Theatre]]'s [[production:2026 Catch Me If You Can|*Catch Me If You Can*]] dazzles audiences in St. Augustine.<!--more-->

In her director's note, [[person:Dawn Knipe]] observes that "in many ways Frank's story feels relevant today because he was carefully crafting identities long before social media existed. Where his methods are far more dramatic, we live in a world where people constantly shape how they present themselves to others online ... the technology has changed, but human nature has not." She invites the audience to "look beyond the disguises and discover the very human story at its heart."

This stellar production delivers on that promise at every turn. Knipe's exquisite direction anchors the show's theatrical flair with emotional resonance, bringing the world of the 1960s to vibrant life. She is backed by a superb creative team: [[person:Jeff Dodd]]'s crisp musical direction keeps the score soaring, [[person:Jonathan Chapman]]'s outstanding choreography brings high-energy spectacle to the stage, and the physical environment, shaped by [[person:Connor Beck]]'s scenic design, [[person:Jenn Michael]]'s fantastic period costumes and [[person:Michael Takita]]'s dynamic lighting design, transports us back to the golden age of jet travel and television.

The ensemble moves seamlessly together, creating striking stage pictures throughout the performance. Leading the cast, [[person:Chad Boyd]] plays the charismatic con artist Frank Abagnale Jr. opposite [[person:David Abraham]] as the relentless FBI agent Carl Hanratty. Boyd commands the stage with effortless charm and a powerful vocal range that makes every number shimmer, balancing Frank's youthful audacity with a vulnerable search for identity. [[person:David Abraham|Abraham]] brings brilliant comedic timing, vocal strength and rich depth to Hanratty, grounding the high-stakes moments.

The story opens in the mid-1960s as Frank finds himself cornered by Hanratty at Miami International Airport. Facing arrest, Frank bargains to explain his journey through a grand musical spectacle, "Live in Living Color." Hanratty agrees, setting off a fast-paced cat-and-mouse chase.

Flashbacks reveal how Frank's reinventions begin. In New York, he forges an ID to pose as a Pan American World Airways co-pilot. The joy of high-flying travel explodes onstage in "Jet Set," a standout, high-octane musical number that highlights the full force of the ensemble, before Frank recalls his father's advice that "women love a man in uniform" in "The Pinstripes Are All That They See."

At the emotional heart of the story is Frank's connection to his parents. [[person:Johnny Jay]] brings charisma and emotional depth to Frank Abagnale Sr., while [[person:Jessica Hardwick]] delivers an elegant performance as Paula Abagnale. When a homesick Frank visits his father and learns he has to close his store to save money, Frank offers forged checks to help. In a poignant moment, Frank Sr. declines, insisting his son focus on his own success rather than worrying about him in "Butter Outta Cream."

At FBI headquarters, Hanratty drowns in a paper trail of fraudulent checks in "Live in Living Color (Reprise)." He lays out his strict philosophy in "Don't Break the Rules," supported by FBI agents played with comedic precision by [[person:Alberto Carlos Peart]], [[person:Matthew Turnage]], [[person:Bryce Esposito]], [[person:Anthony Romero]] and [[person:Max Haddock]].

As Hanratty searches Frank's leftover belongings in "The Man Inside the Clues," the chase reaches Los Angeles. Frank meets the glamorous Cheryl Ann, played with great comedic timing by [[person:Via Grendzinski]], before narrowly escaping Hanratty by pretending to be Secret Service agent "Barry Allen."

Act Two opens with Frank adopting yet another identity, this time posing as Dr. Connors, a pediatrician at Death Valley Children's Hospital, after stumbling into a hospital staff party. He secures a job at Atlanta General Hospital, where he is surrounded by a lively nursing staff ready to take "Doctor's Orders." This show-stopping number showcases the power of the female ensemble, led with incredible vocal strength and acting by [[person:Ashlynn Amburgey]], [[person:Via Grendzinski]], [[person:Rose Roberts]], [[person:Ava Marshall]], [[person:Jayna Giese]] and [[person:Bridgid Mullen]].

It is at the hospital where Frank meets nurse Brenda Strong, portrayed with touching sincerity by [[person:Malia Jaffe]]. Though initially intimidated by Frank after he boasts of seeing the "Seven Wonders of the World," Brenda is swept off her feet as Frank tries to prove that none compare to her. Frank travels to New Orleans to meet her parents, Carol and Roger Strong, exquisitely played by [[person:Lindsay Kozlowski]] and [[person:Rick Cirucci]], who deliver delightful comic warmth during the family television sing-along, "(Our) Family Tree." Though Roger sees through Frank's tall tales, he grants permission for Frank to marry Brenda after witnessing the genuine love Frank holds for his daughter.

Later in Act Two, [[person:Malia Jaffe|Jaffe]] delivers one of the production's highlights with a beautiful rendition of "Fly, Fly Away," supported by gorgeous vocal harmonies from [[person:Ashlynn Amburgey|Amburgey]], [[person:Via Grendzinski|Grendzinski]] and [[person:Rose Roberts|Roberts]] that elevate the emotional peak of the show.

As the narrative loops back to the opening scene at Miami International Airport, the illusion finally strips away. Cornered and realizing he ultimately has nobody left to turn to, Frank surrenders to Hanratty with the explosive 11 o'clock number "Goodbye." Boyd delivers this climax with extraordinary vocal power and razor-sharp wit, bringing his journey full circle in an unforgettable moment of theatrical brilliance.

Even through the final moments, the production's visual genius continues. The curtain call looks absolutely fabulous, serving as a striking visual distillation of the entire story that reveals the many layers of Frank's journey all at once. Without giving away every twist and turn, this phenomenal cast, soaring music and magnificent stage pictures combine to take the audience on an unforgettable journey. Limelight Theatre's *Catch Me If You Can* is a must-see event. I hear it's mostly sold out, but give a call if you can.

***Author's Note:*** *Having worked on the Broadway production team, costume and wardrobe crew for "Catch Me If You Can," returning to these songs is a special treat. Seeing the show come to life on stage again brings back wonderful memories!*
