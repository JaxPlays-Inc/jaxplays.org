[[theatre:Greenlight Theatre Company]]'s [[production:2026 The Hunchback of Notre Dame|*The Hunchback of Notre Dame*]] soars with elegance, dimension and vision. The show opens with an atmospheric and enthralling rendition of "The Bells of Notre Dame," thoughtfully staged by director and choreographer [[person:Christopher Milligan]].

<!--more-->

Milligan, alongside artistic director [[person:Rodney Holmes]], modifies the venue's stage space, expanding the performance area to create a wider, more immersive experience for the audience. As Milligan notes, his vision "embraces both the grandeur and the intimacy of this story. The world of Notre Dame is massive; the bells, the music, the architecture, the spectacle; but within that world are deeply human stories of love, fear, faith, prejudice, courage, and ultimately, the search for freedom."

The stage is put to excellent use by the production team, featuring a 35-member cast, an eight-member choir and a six-piece orchestra under the direction of conductor [[person:Brandon Smith]]. Additional choreography by [[person:Emily Sullivan]], costume design by [[person:Lauren Massais]] and seamless stage management by [[person:Hailee Ketterer]] round out a stellar creative team. Throughout both acts, the large-ensemble scenes are expertly crafted, making the world feel realized and captivating, from the grand opening to the engrossing energy of "In a Place of Miracles" in the Court of Miracles.

The opening sequence sets a powerful tone, anchored by exceptional performances from [[person:Ricky Fields]] as Claude Frollo and [[person:Jack Davis]] as his brother, Jehan. Fields brings immense vocal gravitas to the role; his deep, thunderous tone commands the room instantly, effortlessly balancing Frollo's terrifying moral authority with a rich, velvety warmth that makes his psychological descent into obsession all the more chilling. The entire company provides striking visual storytelling as the story unfolds: orphaned brothers are taken in by Notre Dame, but their paths soon diverge. Jehan grows mischievous and is expelled for his wild lifestyle, while Frollo remains pious and ascends to archdeacon. Years later, a dying Jehan entrusts Frollo with his infant. Frollo, feeling tested by God, saves the child, names him Quasimodo, which means "half-formed," and vows to raise him hidden away in the cathedral.

In the present-day narrative, set in 1482, a grown Quasimodo, partially deafened by a lifetime of ringing the cathedral bells, confides his longing to go "Out There" and attend the Feast of Fools to the stone gargoyles and statues of saints. [[person:Terry Starr|Terry Star]] delivers an intricate, nuanced and breathtaking performance in the title role. Star's mastery of the character rests in his physicalization and vocal choices; he embodies Quasimodo's physical limitations with a grounded commitment that never feels like a caricature, while seamlessly contrasting a strained, bell-damaged speaking voice with a soaring, remarkable singing tone. The statues and gargoyles are expertly played by [[person:EllaMae Turknett]], [[person:Zaria Hood-Washington]], [[person:Silvana Perez Castillo]], [[person:Mya Smoak]], [[person:Elizabeth Schurko]] and [[person:Rowan Gilmore]]. Each performer brings nuanced choices that Star responds to, creating moments that feel remarkably fresh and earnest.

From Quasimodo's longing in "Out There," the action moves to the bustling streets of Paris, anchored by a trio of standout performances. [[person:Maddie Higdon]] commands the stage as Clopin, leading the ensemble with captivating flair, vibrant charisma and sharp theatrical energy. [[person:Aaron Burdick]] brings a commanding presence and vocal strength to Captain Phoebus, striking a compelling balance between military authority and grounded charm. Meanwhile, [[person:Caroline Yazdiya]] delivers a fierce performance as Esmeralda, anchored by a powerful, clear voice full of emotional resonance. They are backed by a dynamic group of Roma dancers: Sullivan, [[person:Caitlin Allen]], [[person:Summer Dees]], [[person:Juliana Williams]], [[person:Carol Yazdiya]] and [[person:Sydney Wissinger]], along with incredible ASL signing and performance from [[person:Reagan Stoker]].

The production's rich tapestry is further strengthened by an ensemble of sharp featured turns, including [[person:Gage Corbett]] as the official, [[person:Daniel Ouellette]] as Saint Aphrodisius, Wissinger as Florika and [[person:Dallas Raulins]] as Father Dupin. All add depth and texture to the world of 15th-century Paris.

Without giving away the full scope of the plot, the production delivers unforgettable peak moments. The soaring, heartfelt staging of "God Help the Outcasts," the charming connection in "Top of the World," the visceral intensity of "Hellfire" and the poignant beauty of "Someday" stand out as true high points of the evening.

Elevating the emotional weight of the story is the outstanding orchestra, led by Smith on piano alongside [[person:Alyssa Albert]] on violin 1, [[person:David Paul Rowan]] on violin 2, [[person:Hannah Barrow]] on viola, [[person:Ian Rivera]] on cello and [[person:Michael Taylor]] on percussion. The incredible choir features [[person:Kate Patterson]], [[person:Erin Wade]], [[person:Hecate Smith]], [[person:Erin Flower]], [[person:Peyton Bennett]], [[person:Luke Hopper]] and [[person:Jarvis Ramil]].

Ultimately, Greenlight Theatre Company delivers a truly sensational and unforgettable show. Beyond its visual and musical brilliance, the production serves as a powerful testament to empathy, reminding us all of the vital need to look past external barriers and recognize the quiet humanity inside others. It is a triumphant theatrical experience that lingers long after the final bells fall silent.
