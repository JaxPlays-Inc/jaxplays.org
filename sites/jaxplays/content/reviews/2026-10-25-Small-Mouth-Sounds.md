In a world where noise is constant and silence is often treated as an empty void to be filled with notifications and endless scrolling, [[theatre:Jacksonville University - Stein College|Jacksonville University]]'s production of [[production:2026 Small Mouth Sounds|*Small Mouth Sounds*]] at [[venue:Swisher Theater]] offers a deeply moving sanctuary. [[person:Thaddeus Walker]]'s direction is masterful, guiding the cast with a vision that uncovers depth within the quiet. Under Walker's stewardship, the movement throughout the piece becomes its own poetic language — every shift in posture, glance and physical release speaks volumes without a single word needed.<!--more-->

As Walker thoughtfully noted about the production's core challenge, "In the world today, people are relying less and less on listening and communication in person, and more on the facade that naturally comes from being online and social media... The actors are learning that they still have to listen and talk to each other even when they can't use words."

Set at a retreat in upstate New York, [[w:Bess Wohl]]'s play follows six urban wanderers who surrender their digital armor under a vow of silence. The movement and design are lovely, working in harmony to transform shared stillness into an immersive theatrical experience. Saturday night's performance was anchored by an exceptional ensemble cast whose rich, nuanced chemistry brought these deeply human characters to vivid life: Vivianna Coppa as Alicia, Sammie Coleman as Joan, Fiona Rushton as Judy, Nathan "Cookie" Cook as Ned, Alexander "Al" Davis as Rodney, Cooper Von Balson as Jan and [[person:Alec Hadden]] as the Voice of the Teacher.

The stellar ensemble performance was matched at every step by an outstanding design and production team. Jelopi Sedeu Nishevikj's atmospheric sound design balanced ambient texture with dramatic weight, while Haleigh Canaday's lighting design guided the mood through poignant revelation. [[person:Abigail Marks]] handled props and stage management with skill, ensuring the tactile details grounded every moment, while Theo Austell's costume and program design added essential visual texture and artful clarity to the evening.

In an era dominated by hyper-connectivity and quick-fix culture, JU's *Small Mouth Sounds* lovingly reminds us that healing begins when we step away from screen-driven distractions and sit with one another. Warm and brilliantly executed, this production is a reminder that even in silence, we are never alone.
