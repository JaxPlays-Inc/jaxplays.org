[[person:Redgie Gutshall]] spent decades on Northeast Florida stages, but the community he leaves behind is remembering him as much for the seat he kept in the front row as for the roles he played in the spotlight.<!--more-->

Gutshall died Sept. 15, 2026, at the age of 79. Born May 1, 1947, he is survived by his wife, [[person:Holly Gutshall]], his frequent scene partner and constant companion in a theatre life the two built together across the region. His care is in the hands of Hardage-Giddens Greenlawn Funeral Home and Cemetery.

[[theatre:Theatre Jacksonville]] called him "one of Jacksonville's outstanding leading men," noting that he graced Northeast Florida stages beginning in the 1980s. Over the years, Redgie and Holly appeared alongside each other and separately in hundreds of productions, including decades of work at [[theatre:Players by the Sea]].

Before he found the stage in Jacksonville, Gutshall studied theatre at Southwest Missouri State College and served a long career in the U.S. Navy, working as an air traffic control officer from 1968 to 1995 — responsible, as the job demands, for the safe and orderly flow of aircraft in his assigned airspace.

In the days since his death, tributes have poured in from theatre companies across the region, each returning to the same theme: a man who loved the work as fiercely from a seat in the house as from center stage.

"Our hearts are incredibly heavy as we mourn the passing of our dear friend Redgie Gutshall, a man whose impact reached far beyond any one theatre, production, or organization," [[theatre:Arts Collective Theatre]] wrote in a tribute. "Redgie was a gifted actor, and those fortunate enough to see him perform knew the talent and heart he brought to the stage. But what made Redgie so special was that his love for theatre didn't end when he wasn't the one standing in the spotlight."

That instinct — to show up for everyone else — is the thread running through nearly every remembrance. Theatre needs actors, directors, designers and audiences, Arts Collective wrote, "but it also needs champions. People who believe that the work matters enough to keep showing up, even when the spotlight isn't on them. Redgie was one of those champions."

Players by the Sea remembered him as "a passionate and irreplaceable member" of its extended family. "To imagine a community where Redgie isn't sitting on the front row, with that wide smile and infectious laugh, is hard," the theatre wrote. "Redgie and his wife, Holly, were ardent participants and supporters throughout the community. Their love for this theatre community knew no bounds."

[[theatre:ABET - All Beaches Experimental Theatre]] echoed the sentiment, calling the Gutshalls "enthusiastic champions of ABET for many years, on and off the stage, and always willing to lend a hand or offer their support whenever it was needed."

Producer and actor [[person:Lee Hamby]], put it plainly: "My heart is broken. Redgie Gutshall was my biggest cheerleader, an amazing friend, a constant supporter, a huge part of our Jacksonville theatre community as an actor and patron, and a damn good guy."

As Arts Collective Theatre wrote in its farewell: "You spent time in the spotlight, but you were just as willing to shine it on others. That may be one of the greatest legacies a theatre person can leave behind."

I met Redgie in 2015, when I was directing WJCT Radio Theater's production of *It's a Wonderful Life* and he played the Superintendent Angel. During one recording session he delivered something so delightfully curmudgeonly that I said as much out loud — and without missing a beat, bone-dry, he shot back, "Well, that's how I got this role, isn't it?" I busted out laughing. Then he busted out laughing, and we had to stop recording until we could both pull ourselves together. In the years since, he was a steadfast supporter of JaxPlays. Every time I ran into him in a theatre, he encouraged me to keep writing and to keep at the work of lifting up this community. He never stopped shining that light on other people.

The community's love, in these first difficult days, has gathered around Holly Gutshall and the family. Details of services had not been announced at the time of publication.
