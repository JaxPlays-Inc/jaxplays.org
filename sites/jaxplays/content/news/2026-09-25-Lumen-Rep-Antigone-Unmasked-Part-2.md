For Dr. [[person:Brian Niece]], artistic director of [[theatre:Lumen Repertory Theatre]], classical drama serves as a living canvas for contemporary reflection rather than a preserved historical artifact. With the premiere of [[production:2026 Antigone: Unmasked]], Lumen Rep presents its fifth original work in four years. In a continuing dialogue with JaxPlays, Niece details a vision focused on developing original productions and building sustainable artistic infrastructure.<!--more-->

**JaxPlays:** As artistic director, how does developing original or newly adapted work fit into your larger vision for Lumen Rep?

**Brian Niece:** Lumen Rep exists to produce work at a professional standard in Jacksonville, and that has to include work made here, by this company, for this city. *Antigone: Unmasked* is our fifth original work in four years, and it serves as proof of concept twice over. We can generate new work and mount it with a fully professional ensemble, and that work doesn't stay local. A company in South Florida is currently mounting our *ShakesBeer* concept, and last year's original piece has real potential at other regional companies. The word "Rep" in Lumen Rep isn't just a name - it's a commitment. We're building a true repertory company, one that develops work and artists year over year, not show to show.

**JaxPlays:** What do you think original work uniquely offers to the local theater community?

**Niece:** It says the work doesn't have to come from somewhere else, and it doesn't have to stay here either. Original work builds local artistic infrastructure: playwrights, directors, companies willing to take the risk, and audiences willing to meet them there. We're already seeing that infrastructure travel, with our *ShakesBeer* concept running in South Florida and last season's original work drawing interest from other regional companies. It also gives NEXTGEN Apprentices something rare: a front-row seat to how a new play actually gets built, from page to stage, rather than just how an existing one gets revived.

**JaxPlays:** What do you hope audiences are still thinking or talking about after they leave the theater?

**Niece:** Where the line is between order and control, and who in their own lives is still wearing a mask they haven't been allowed to take off. Maybe what constitutes their own masks. Also, what is truth? That may sound like a philosophical question, and it is, but it's vitally practical as well. This one comes from a personal place too, not only a scholarly or theatrical one. I've been married to my wife for 28 years, and two of our three children are daughters. I'm still learning how not to be the voice that quiets theirs. I've watched my wife, in career and social spaces alike, be told without anyone saying it outright to sit down and be quiet, simply because she's a woman. There's something ingrained in us, chosen rather than natural, that keeps women's voices from being heard the way men's are, and I've had to look hard at my own words and habits and keep working toward stepping aside instead of stepping over. I hope audiences leave examining their own history with the women's voices in their lives - whose they've listened to and whose they haven't, and why.

**JaxPlays:** What do you hope this production says about where you are as an artist, and where you'd like Lumen Rep to go next?

**Niece:** As an artist, I've been in dialogue with the classical tradition for a while, but now I'm writing from inside it. Though I've written and co-written original works in the past, completing five original works in four years with Lumen Rep means this is no longer a side project - it's the center of what I do. I've been an Actors' Equity Association member for over 25 years, and I know the difference between rooms that take artists seriously and rooms that don't. Lumen Rep is built to be the former. Every member of this production's team, onstage and off, is compensated. Hiring an Equity actor for this production wasn't a box to check; it was a statement about what professional standards mean here. As for where we go next, the "Rep" becomes literal - a true repertory ensemble built year over year. We're generating work that starts in Jacksonville without staying confined to it, while NEXTGEN apprentices grow into the professionals who carry that mission forward.

### Author's Reflection

This line of discussion hits at a vital truth. In my doctoral capstone at Vanderbilt University, I examined the visibility of women's voices in the performing arts, conducting qualitative interviews with 16 female leaders spanning from Broadway to regional companies. The research documented systemic challenges, but living it presents an even clearer picture. Niece's willingness to confront these dynamics brings a necessary, candid conversation from the stage into the community.

### Production Details

A re-examination of Sophocles' classic tragedy interrogates modern dynamics of authority, identity and public performance in *Antigone: Unmasked* - a thematic choice rooted as much in experience as scholarly rigor. Through the lens of the production, Niece hopes audiences leave the theater reflecting on the boundaries between order and control, the masks they wear, and whose voices they choose to validate.

*Antigone: Unmasked* runs Oct. 9-24, 2026, at [[venue:Betsy Lovett Hall]] in the Church of the Good Shepherd, 1100 Stockton St., Jacksonville. For tickets, show times and additional information, visit [lumenrep.org](https://www.lumenrep.org/).
