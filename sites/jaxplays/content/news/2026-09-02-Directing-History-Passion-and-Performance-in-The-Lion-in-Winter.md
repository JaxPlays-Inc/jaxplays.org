There is a distinct synergy when history and high drama collide on the local stage. As [[theatre:Theatre Jacksonville]] brings James Goldman's masterpiece, *[[production:2026 The Lion in Winter|The Lion in Winter]]*, to life, the production stands as a master class in theatrical storytelling, dramaturgy and character development. At the center of this majestic storm is director [[person:Jeff Grove]] — a visionary whose guidance and passion continue to leave an enduring mark on Jacksonville's arts and educational community.<!--more-->

For many of us in the local theatre scene, working alongside Jeff is a full-circle blessing. Having first experienced his mentorship during my high school years at Stanton College Preparatory School, I find that witnessing him take the helm for this royal showdown is an absolute joy. Grove brings a rare blend of rigorous dramaturgical insight for the actor's craft, fostering an educational and creative rehearsal environment where classic works feel vibrantly alive.

Grove shares, "Every great production needs a compass, and for this staging of *The Lion in Winter*, our primary guiding light comes directly from the playwright's own note at the head of the script." That note reads:

> The historical material on Henry's reign is considerable insofar as battles, plots, wars, treaties, and alliances are concerned. This play—while simplifying the political maneuvering by combining a meeting of the French and English kings in 1183 with a Royal Court held at Windsor in the following year into a Christmas Court that never was—is accurately based on the available data.
>
> The facts we have, while clear enough as to the outcome of relationships—such things as who kills whom and when—say little if anything about the quality and content of those relationships. The people in this play, their characters and passions, while consistent with the facts we have, are fictitious. The play, finally, contains anachronisms in speech, thought, habit, custom and so on. Those the author is aware of—the way, for instance, Christmas is celebrated—are deliberate and not intended to outrage the historical aspects of the script.

Goldman's commentary gives the Theatre Jacksonville creative team license to explore the volatile space between recorded facts and human truths. Under Jeff Grove's direction, the team creates a "generalized, fairy-tale medieval world—a delicate balance that combines grounded historical accuracy with deliberate, theatrical adjustments designed to heighten emotional stakes and resonate" with modern audiences. In untangling the legendary Plantagenet family, history gives us the what and the when, but dramaturgy allows us to discover the why.

While Henry II, Eleanor of Aquitaine and their ambitious sons are bound by real treaties, crowns and betrayals, their fiercely human passions are forged right here in the rehearsal room. Grove's directorial approach treats character development as an intimate exploration of love, political chess and family dynamics that feel strikingly contemporary. Theatre at its best is a continuous educational journey, both for the artists on stage and the audience in the seats. By embracing the rich theatricality, this production invites us to look past the stone walls and crown jewels to find the beating heart of human connection.

As Theatre Jacksonville raises the curtain on this legendary royal court, local theatre lovers experience a production that honors both historical scope and pure dramatic fire. Guided by Jeff Grove's experienced hand and deep commitment to artistic excellence, *The Lion in Winter* is an unmissable theatrical event for our community.

**Event details**
- **Dates:** Sept. 19-Oct. 4, 2026
- **Location:** Theatre Jacksonville, 2032 San Marco Blvd., Jacksonville, Fla.
- **Plot:** Set during Christmas 1183, the story follows King Henry II, the imprisoned Queen Eleanor of Aquitaine and their sons as they fight fiercely over royal succession, land and power.
- **Tone:** A witty, dramatic and darkly comedic look at historic family dysfunction.
