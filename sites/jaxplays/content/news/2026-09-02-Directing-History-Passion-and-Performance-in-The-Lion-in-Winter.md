A distinct synergy emerges whenever history and high drama collide on stage. Marking the grand opening of [[theatre:Theatre Jacksonville]]'s 107th season, James Goldman's *[[production:2026 The Lion in Winter|The Lion in Winter]]* brings a complex study of power and human passion to San Marco. Directed by [[person:Jeff Grove]], a longtime local educator and director, the production features an ensemble cast including Rebeque Destro, Eli Bruce Durham, Collin Galvin, Kenny Logsdon, Dustin Ponder, Sydney Wissinger and Aidan Wood.<!--more-->

Rather than treating the script purely as a period piece, Grove grounds the creative process in historical and text-based research. Drawing on his background in education and stage direction, he balances period context with the physical and psychological demands placed on the cast, turning the rehearsal room into an active conversational space of discovery.

"Every great production needs a compass," Grove shares, "and for this staging of *The Lion in Winter*, our primary guiding light comes directly from the playwright's own note at the head of the script." Goldman writes in the script:

> The historical material on Henry's reign is considerable insofar as battles, plots, wars, treaties, and alliances are concerned. This play—while simplifying the political maneuvering by combining a meeting of the French and English kings in 1183 with a Royal Court held at Windsor in the following year into a Christmas Court that never was—is accurately based on the available data.
>
> The facts we have, while clear enough as to the outcome of relationships—such things as who kills whom and when—say little if anything about the quality and content of those relationships. The people in this play, their characters and passions, while consistent with the facts we have, are fictitious. The play, finally, contains anachronisms in speech, thought, habit, custom and so on. Those the author is aware of—the way, for instance, Christmas is celebrated—are deliberate and not intended to outrage the historical aspects of the script.

Goldman's commentary gives the creative team license to explore the space between recorded facts and human truths. Grove notes that the artistic team works to build a "generalized, fairy-tale medieval world" — a delicate balance that combines grounded historical accuracy with theatrical adjustments designed to heighten emotional stakes for modern audiences. In untangling the legendary Plantagenet family, history provides the what and the when, but dramaturgy allows the ensemble to discover the why.

While Henry II, Eleanor of Aquitaine and their ambitious sons remain bound by historical treaties, crowns and betrayals, their human passions take shape daily in the rehearsal room. Grove's direction frames character development as an intimate exploration of timeless family dynamics. As Theatre Jacksonville opens its 107th season on the MainStage, this production offers a thoughtful look at how dramaturgy and collaborative direction bring classic dramatic literature to life for local theatergoers.

**Event details**
- **Dates:** Sept. 19-Oct. 4, 2026
- **Location:** Theatre Jacksonville, 2032 San Marco Blvd., Jacksonville, Fla.
- **Cast:** Rebeque Destro, Eli Bruce Durham, Collin Galvin, Kenny Logsdon, Dustin Ponder, Sydney Wissinger and Aidan Wood
- **Plot:** Set during Christmas 1183, the story follows King Henry II, the imprisoned Queen Eleanor of Aquitaine and their sons as they fight over royal succession, land and power.
- **Tone:** A witty, dramatic and darkly comedic look at historic family dysfunction.
