Ahead of opening weekend for [[production:2026 Carrie The Musical|*Carrie: The Musical*]], JaxPlays spoke with choreographer [[person:Terry Starr|Terry Star]] about how movement breathes emotion into the iconic story.<!--more-->

In musical theatre, dance is more than background spectacle. It can reveal the fears, desires and subtext that characters struggle to express in words. Star discussed capturing the anxiety of high school, staging the show's key moments and using dance as a lens into Carrie White's inner world and the shifting dynamics among her classmates.

**JaxPlays: Dance and movement can reveal what characters struggle to express in words. How have you used choreography to communicate Carrie's isolation, her desire to belong and the shifting power dynamics among her classmates?**

**Terry Star:** There are moments in the show where, even if all of the high schoolers are onstage, including Carrie, she is in a different setting than the rest of them. While everyone is excited about prom and getting ready together, she is in her own home wishing she could be with the rest of them. In the moments where we're inside Carrie's mind, the other kids are there, but only as a representation of her insecurities and self-doubt.

**JaxPlays: The high school setting brings its own physical language, from social hierarchies and peer pressure to the energy of teenage friendships and rivalries. How did you use movement to establish those relationships and make the world of the school feel authentic?**

**Star:** Throughout the show, there are plenty of moments where I gave choreography to those portraying the high schoolers and told them to come up with their own interpretation of it. I told them to remember what they were like in high school, or how it felt, and to use that in their physicality and facial expressions.

**JaxPlays: This musical blends the familiar rhythms of teenage life with horror and psychological tension. How do you balance recognizable movement with more unsettling or unexpected physical choices as the story darkens?**

**Star:** There are moments in the show where we are "inside" Carrie's mind. I used those moments to convey the unsettling horror feeling that is needed for this show. There are also moments of foreshadowing throughout the show that let the audience know something bad is going to happen.

**JaxPlays: With a show like *Carrie*, ensemble movement can become a powerful storytelling force in its own right. How have you collaborated with the director and music director to ensure the choreography, music and staging work together to build tension and propel the story forward?**

**Star:** *Carrie* is a type of show that does not have many moments for a "dance break," so it took a lot of collaboration with the director to figure out which moments in the show were appropriate for choreography. There are moments where I specifically requested that the music be slowed down because it elevates the storytelling in such a beautiful way. Our music director has done an amazing job making sure the choreography is conveyed the way it needs to be.

**JaxPlays: What has been the most challenging or rewarding choreographic moment in this production? Was there a particular scene where the movement evolved in an unexpected way once you began working with the cast?**

**Star:** The most rewarding moment had to be working with the cast on the destruction. I was nervous going into that rehearsal because there's no fun way of explaining how each of them dies onstage. After teaching them the destruction and seeing the cast run it a few times, I realized what a great show we have. They took what I taught them and turned it into something amazing.

**JaxPlays: For audiences who may know *Carrie* primarily through its horror legacy, what do you hope they discover about the characters and the emotional heart of the musical through your choreography?**

**Star:** Most audiences know the shocking ending of this story and how it affects Carrie herself, but with the choreography in this show, I hope audiences get to see how everyone in that school is just a kid at the end of the day, and they make mistakes. Prom is when you realize all these teenagers are just excited for their senior prom, and the choreography highlights their excitement while making the ending even more tragic.
