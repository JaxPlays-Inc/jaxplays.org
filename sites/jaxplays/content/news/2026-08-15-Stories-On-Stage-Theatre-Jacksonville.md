As a professor of theatre for the last 10 years, I speak often about the crucial importance of being heard, experiencing new work and building genuine community. Theatre, at its core, is not just about polished mainstage productions; it is about creating a living space where new voices find their courage and audiences gather to really listen. That is precisely why *Stories on Stage* matters so deeply to our local arts landscape.<!--more-->

At [[theatre:Theatre Jacksonville]], one of Northeast Florida's oldest community theatres, the stage almost always hums with a mainstage production. Yet nestled alongside that busy season is *Stories on Stage*, a vital storytelling project. Designed for people who want to step onstage, the initiative offers a welcoming gateway for anyone itching to try something new without the commitment of a full theatrical run, ensuring a wide variety of cultivated and creative opportunities for the entire community.

[[person:Katie Johnston]] hosts the program, bringing the rich perspective of a seasoned performer who understands firsthand what it takes to step into the spotlight. As both partner and shepherd to these creative sparks, she offers gentle guidance grounded in real theatrical experience. She fields submissions, curates the lineup, answers logistical questions and works one-on-one with performers to refine their work until it is performance-ready.

Four times a year, the theatre sets aside a Sunday evening to celebrate the myriad ways people connect through performance. The format feels like a casual, intimate variety show running 60 to 75 minutes with no intermission. By keeping the house at half-light, the audience and performers sit together in direct conversation, creating a safe, accessible space to experiment.

Personal stories, scenes from plays, original sketches, songs, short film screenings and staged readings of works-in-progress all find a home here. Performances work around whatever mainstage set currently sits onstage, keeping spatial and technical needs simple so the focus stays entirely on the human element. This series bridges the gap between Jacksonville's many artistic bubbles, connecting diverse local talent and creative collectives under one roof. The program is open to any and all who want to submit or participate.

### Upcoming Events and How to Get Involved

The momentum continues Sunday, Aug. 16, with the third event of the series, "Far From Home," at 7:30 p.m.

Looking further ahead, the program gears up for its final showcase of the year Sunday, Nov. 15, at 7:30 p.m. The finale features an "Anything Goes" ethos: no theme, no restrictions and complete creative freedom. Submissions for the November event remain open through Oct. 15.

Whether you pull up a chair to listen, test out a short film, workshop a new play or step onto a stage for the very first time, *Stories on Stage* opens the doors and hands you the microphone.
