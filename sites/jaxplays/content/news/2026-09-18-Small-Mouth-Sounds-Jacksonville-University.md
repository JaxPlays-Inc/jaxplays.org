JACKSONVILLE, Fla. - Silence is often treated as an absence, a void waiting to be filled by notifications, curated feeds and chatter. But in [[theatre:Jacksonville University - Stein College|Jacksonville University]]'s upcoming production of *[[production:2026 Small Mouth Sounds|Small Mouth Sounds]]*, taking place at [[venue:Swisher Theater]], silence serves as the main stage.<!--more-->

Directed by [[person:Thaddeus Walker]], the production runs Oct. 22 and Oct. 23 at 7:30 p.m., with two performances Oct. 24 at 2 p.m. and 7:30 p.m.

Set at a quiet retreat in upstate New York, *[[production:2026 Small Mouth Sounds|Small Mouth Sounds]]* follows six urban runaways seeking sanctuary, clarity and healing. Guided by a disembodied teacher whose voice booms over the grounds, the characters navigate personal grief and desperation under a strict vow of silence.

Written by 2026 Pulitzer Prize and Tony Award-winning playwright [[w:Bess Wohl]] (*Liberation*), the play stems from a real-life silent retreat Wohl attended in upstate New York. First developed at Ars Nova in 2015, the work established Wohl as a prominent voice in contemporary theater. By stripping away traditional dialogue, Wohl crafts a study of vulnerability that relies heavily on physicality, posture and unspoken emotional exchange.

The play's core arrives at a pivotal moment in modern culture. As wellness industries expand into a trillion-dollar global market, audiences increasingly seek depth over superficial trends. The wellness surge often promises quick escapes, yet Wohl's narrative explores what happens when people drop their digital armor and sit with their unvarnished selves.

"In the world today, people are relying less and less on listening and communication in person, and more on the facade that naturally comes from being online and social media," Walker says. "As we explore in rehearsal, the actors are learning that they still have to listen and talk to each other even when they can't use words. Just the other day, I told two actors they were gesturing at each other but not paying attention to how those gestures were affecting the other person."

That quiet tension directly reflects the play's core challenge to the audience: when did you last allow yourself to simply sit in stillness?

"I think the way this show uses silence can be very thought-provoking," Walker says. "When is the last time you just sat with someone in silence for minutes on end without reaching for a phone or tablet?"

To deepen the production's exploration of human behavior, Walker introduces a dynamic staging approach inside Swisher Theater. The majority of the cast doubles up on roles across the four-show run, ensuring that no two performances are identical.

"For me, the biggest thing is that these are all real people," Walker says. "At the front of the script, the playwright gives almost page-long character descriptions, which is incredibly rare, so from the start, the actor has a better understanding of who this person is. I've also challenged the actors by having most of them double up on characters. It creates more of a learning opportunity, but also gives the unique opportunity for each show to be different. There are four performances, which means that every audience sees a different casting variation."

Ultimately, *Small Mouth Sounds* invites viewers to re-examine how they define human connection in an era dominated by hyper-connectivity.

"The teacher in the show says on day one, 'Once you see the ocean, you may not go back to the well,'" Walker says. "In the show, it's used as a way to tell the characters that their lives up to that point are a 'small well,' but there is a vast ocean still waiting to be seen. I think so often we find ourselves comparing our 'wells' to someone else's just because social media makes it so easy. The ocean is out there, and I hope it's found in our connections with our family and friends, the fulfillment of our jobs or hobbies, and not a like button."

By stepping away from screen-driven distractions, theatergoers are encouraged to step into the shared space of Swisher Theater.
