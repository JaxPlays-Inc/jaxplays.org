As America approaches its 250th anniversary, [[theatre:Theatre Troupe 1776]] is returning to Revolutionary War history with [[production:2026 The Price of Liberty|The Price of Liberty]], a new historical play by Louise Everett.<!--more-->

The production, directed by [[person:Patty Katez Everett|Patty Everett]], follows patriot and loyalist families from the beginning of the war to its end, asking what freedom costs when the conflict reaches kitchens, siblings and neighbors.

JaxPlays spoke with Louise Everett and Patty Everett about the creative journey, collaborative process and historical vision behind the production.

## Part I: The Playwright's Vision with Louise Everett

**JaxPlays: What first sparked the idea for this play? Was there a particular moment, person or story that stayed with you?**

**Louise Everett:** We knew we wanted to do a show set during the American Revolution in honor of the 250th, but the original idea was for a "colonial Christmas show." After researching for a bit, I realized there was too much history from 1775 to 1783 to pick exactly what year I wanted the story set in. So I decided to write about what a family's life would be like from the beginning of the war to the very end.

**JaxPlays: What drew you to these characters? Did you discover them as you were writing, or did you know them from the beginning?**

**Everett:** I knew I wanted one patriot family to be the center of the story, but as I was writing I got the idea to include a second family who were loyalists to offer a different point of view. While writing them I wondered what it would be like if one of the brothers in the loyalist family didn't want to be a loyalist. The idea of two brothers fighting on opposite sides of the American Revolution really interested me, and then the scenes began to come easily after that.

**JaxPlays: Did the play take you somewhere you didn't expect?**

**Everett:** As I said, the original idea was to focus on a patriot family. The loyalist family was not meant to become as important to the story as they did but I found I really enjoyed writing them and their relationship with the patriot family too much to keep them in the background.

**JaxPlays: Is there something about this story that feels especially personal or meaningful to you?**

**Everett:** The character names are very meaningful to me. I love history and learning family history. While doing this, I researched a lot of my own family history during this time, and I took names from my own ancestors to create these characters.

**JaxPlays: What do you hope audiences take away from the experience? Is there a feeling or question you hope stays with them after they leave the theatre?**

**Everett:** The main point of *The Price of Liberty* is the price that these men and women paid to give us the freedom we often take for granted. In the age of social media, our beliefs can become very black and white. *The Price of Liberty* shows that at the end of the day, we are all people who have our own opinions and believe what we think is right.

**JaxPlays: What has it been like seeing your words come to life onstage? Is there a moment that feels particularly magical, or completely different from how you imagined it?**

**Everett:** The first read through is always particularly magical to me. Up until that point I am always stressed about who will come to auditions, did I write it the best way I can, are people going to like it, etc. Giving the cast the script and hearing them react positively to what has lived only in my head is a bit of a sigh of relief. And seeing them all become the characters that were only figments of my imagination is very, very cool.

**JaxPlays: How has working with this theatre company shaped the play? Have the actors or creative team brought anything to the story that surprised you?**

**Everett:** I think the thing that surprised me the most was how perfectly the actors fit the roles. There's always a mini panic before auditions in my head that we won't find the "perfect person," but it always comes together in the end.

**JaxPlays: Was there a particular character or moment that was especially challenging to write?**

**Everett:** George Washington was the most challenging to write. As a history major and a history nerd in general, I am a big fan of George Washington. He is the only real person in the entire production, meaning he was the one I knew I had to get right. I read a lot of books about him and one that was a compilation of some letters he wrote. I did not want to add my own thoughts and ideas to a real person; that would defeat the whole point of having him there. Hopefully he'd be happy with the way I wrote him.

**JaxPlays: What have you learned about yourself as a playwright through this process?**

**Everett:** I think I've learned I'm more of a perfectionist than I thought. I spent a ridiculous number of hours researching the era, Washington, battles, etc. I even roped my friend, McKenna Rowell, who writes historical fiction, into fact checking everything for me. But when you love something this much, you want to do it right.

**JaxPlays: For someone seeing your work for the first time, what would you love them to know?**

**Everett:** History isn't boring. Not even a little bit. I think in school we get a little bored by the endless memorizing of dates that we cannot possibly remember. History isn't just memorizing a bunch of dates. History is the stories of ordinary people doing incredible things. You don't have to know everything about every era to enjoy history. I hope people seeing this show realize that the American Revolution was not simply some battles in a history book but real people making huge sacrifices so that we can have all the freedoms we have today.

## Part II: Directing the Stage with Patty Everett

**JaxPlays: How did the idea to tackle this specific period come together for the company?**

**Patty Everett:** All of our plays have been focused on a particular era. When we were planning our fall show, we felt like we should do something in honor of America's 250th! Since our theatre troupe was born after our 2023 show, *Unmasked*, which was about spies in the Revolutionary War, we decided it was a good time to return to our roots!

**JaxPlays: What has been the most exciting aspect of bringing this historical setting to life?**

**Patty Everett:** My favorite part of this production has been blending fictional characters with a real historical figure, George Washington. The playwright has an extensive knowledge of Washington, and attempting to show his real character on the stage was the goal. Most people think of Washington as our first president. We are representing him at an earlier time.

**JaxPlays: How does the collaborative process work between you, the playwright and the cast?**

**Patty Everett:** The relationship of this theatre troupe is unique and unlike any that I have ever experienced in my theatre career. The playwright discusses the show with me as she is writing it. When it comes time for auditions, she provides character descriptions that include key personality traits.

At the first table read, we discuss these characters at length, and actors are able to meet with the playwright directly to explore them, something very unique to our troupe. During the rehearsal period, actors can ask for specifics on their roles, and the production team is involved from the very start. Everyone has access to myself and the playwright so that we can bring the true vision of the show to the stage. To complete the experience, our hospitality manager even decorates the house and prepares show-themed snacks.

**JaxPlays: Have there been any memorable moments during rehearsals that stood out to you?**

**Patty Everett:** I always enjoy seeing the actors dive deeper into their characters. At a recent rehearsal, an actor asked which hand was the character's dominant hand. We all got a chuckle, but he was asking because the character becomes injured in the war. He had great insight into his role.

**JaxPlays: What do you hope the audience experiences when they come to see the show?**

**Patty Everett:** Most importantly, we want audiences to walk away with a true sense of what was happening in America in 1776 and beyond. The playwright did a truly amazing job of representing the families and soldiers of that time. It was neighbor against neighbor and even brother against brother, all fighting for their cause.

I want audiences to gain a genuine understanding of the period's history through these fictional characters. My goal for all of my productions is that the audience gets lost in our story and really feels as though they've stepped back in time. We strive for period-accurate costumes, props and set pieces, and I hope they enjoy the production as much as we have enjoyed creating it.
